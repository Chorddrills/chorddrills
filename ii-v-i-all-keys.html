Practise ii–V–I in all 12 keys

The ii–V–I is the most common chord progression in jazz. In C major it is Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7. If you can play it in all twelve keys without stopping to think, a large part of the jazz repertoire opens up. Chord Drills is built for exactly this kind of repetition.

What the ii–V–I is

The chords sit on the second, fifth and first degree of a major scale. The ii (Dm7) leads to the V (G7), which pulls strongly towards the I (Cmaj7). The minor version uses the same idea in a minor key: Dm7♭5 – G7♭9 – Cm7 (or Cm6) in C minor.

Why all 12 keys?

Tunes change key all the time. A progression you only know in C is not much use when the song modulates to E♭ or F♯. Practising it in every key makes the shapes and the sound automatic.

How to practise it in Chord Drills

  • Progressions mode: pick II–V–I (major) or II–V–I (minor) and the keys you want. Jazz progressions start with seventh chords automatically.
  • Repeat button: the loop button next to the key name repeats the same progression until you stop it. “Next” still brings a new task.
  • Key journey (endless): the progression moves from key to key without end. You choose how the key moves: up a fourth (circle of fifths), down a whole step, up a minor third, random keys and more.
  • Two chords alternating: loop Dm7 ↔ G7 as a vamp. Buttons transpose both chords up or down by a half step, and a small analysis shows how the two chords relate: common tones, the keys they share and a possible reading such as “G7 is the dominant of Cmaj7”.
  • Display: show degrees (II, V, I), chord names, or both. Switch between them any time.

A practice plan

  1. One key, slowly, root-position chords.
  2. Shell voicings: only the third and the seventh, with the root in the left hand.
  3. Rootless voicings (an option lets you leave out the root).
  4. All 12 keys through the circle of fourths, one key per day or the whole cycle.
  5. The minor ii–V–i.
  6. Add the metronome with a jazz groove and raise the tempo step by step.

Chord Drills checks which notes you play, not how well you voice them. It is a drill tool and not a course, so combine it with a good lesson or book. Related: random chord generator.
