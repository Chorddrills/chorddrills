Chord inversions: learn and practise

An inversion is the same chord with a different note at the bottom. The notes do not change, only their order. Inversions let you move smoothly from chord to chord without jumping around the keyboard, and they are the foundation of good voice leading.

The inversions of Cmaj7

  • Root position: C E G B
  • 1st inversion: E G B C, written Cmaj7/E
  • 2nd inversion: G B C E, written Cmaj7/G
  • 3rd inversion: B C E G, written Cmaj7/B

A triad has two inversions, a seventh chord has three. The note after the slash is the bass note, the lowest note you play.

Why practise them?

Playing a chord in root position is only the first step. In real music you choose the inversion that is closest to the previous chord. For that you need to know every inversion of every chord by heart and be able to find it quickly.

How to practise in Chord Drills

  • Drill mode: in Random chords choose the position: any position, root position, or the 1st, 2nd and 3rd inversion, and you can mix them. The task shows the slash chord, for example Cmaj7/E.
  • The bass rule: your lowest note has to be the bass note, so you really play the inversion. The other notes may be in any octave and order.
  • Learn mode: in the learning section choose a chord, a root and an inversion. You see the name, the notes with intervals, the keyboard and the notation for each inversion. Seventh chords show all four positions.
  • Limits: extended chords (9th, 11th, 13th) have no inversions here and are played in any position.

A practice plan

  1. Pick one chord type, for example major triads, and play all inversions in all 12 keys.
  2. Switch to seventh chords and do the same.
  3. Choose only one inversion at a time, for example only the 1st inversion across all roots.
  4. Link two chords by playing the second one in the inversion closest to the first. The progression trainer is good for this.
  5. Use time attack to check how fast you can find the right voicing.
